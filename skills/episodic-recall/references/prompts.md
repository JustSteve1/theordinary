# Conversation templates

Adapt wording to the person's register. Keep each message short. Never reveal window content
before free recall is finished.

## Consent (Phase 0)

> I can run a short memory check-in based on your own day. Here's what it involves: I'll quietly
> read what Bee captured yesterday to build an answer key, then ask you to tell me about part of
> the day in your own words. Nothing you say is judged against anyone else — only against your own
> previous sessions. I'll store a score and the date locally, plus one short summary line in your
> Bee facts. I won't store what other people said. This isn't a medical test and I won't be
> diagnosing anything. Want to go ahead?

If no → "No problem. I won't read anything." End.

## Confounders (Phase 0)

> Two quick ones first, just yes or no: did you sleep badly last night? And are you feeling unwell
> today, or did you have a few drinks yesterday?

## Free recall (Phase 3)

Opening (choose by window):

> Take your time. Tell me everything you can remember about yesterday **afternoon** — who you were
> with, what you talked about, what you did, where you were. Rough is fine; I'll just listen.

Follow-up 1 (after they stop): > Anything else?

Follow-up 2: > Anything else at all, even small things?

Then: > Thanks, that's everything I need. Give me a moment to score it.

Do **not** say "good", "right", "yes", "exactly", or react to specific items.

## Feedback (Phase 7)

Structure:

1. One sentence on what came back strongly, by type ("You remembered everyone you spoke to and
   the main topics").
2. The composite, framed as a score out of 100 for *this* session.
3. Trend: either "This is session N of 5 before we have a baseline to compare against" or
   "That's close to / a bit above / a bit below your usual range" using `z`.
4. Confounders, if either was yes: "You mentioned poor sleep, which can affect this, so I've
   noted that against today's score."
5. Offer: "Want me to show you the things from the afternoon that didn't come up?"

If `flag` is `review_suggested`:

> A few recent sessions have come in below your usual range. On its own that doesn't mean much
> and there are lots of everyday reasons for it, but it's the kind of pattern worth mentioning to
> your GP next time you see them. Would you like me to pull together the dates and scores so you
> have them?

Never use: normal/abnormal, impaired, decline, deficit, dementia, Alzheimer's, MCI, diagnosis.

## Abort

> Let's leave it there — no score for today, and nothing's been saved. We can try another time
> if you'd like.
